Notes

  • The profile is the STEP's, not an approximation. All 28 segments — 14 lines and 14 arcs — were read from the AP242 file and rebuilt. Checked against a FreeCAD mesh of the STEP: the two solids differ by 0.0012 % in volume and no point of either surface lies more than 0.001 mm from the other.
  • Everything is 3 mm. Slot width, arm width and web width are identical, so each free end is a half-round of that diameter. Every corner is R1.5 apart from the two at the closed end, which are R3.
  • G1 and G2 are a half bridge, bonded back to back on the central column and centred 3.5 mm below the plate centre. The two faces of a bending member strain equally and oppositely, so in adjacent bridge arms the pair adds — while any axial load, which strains both faces alike, cancels out.
  • Why that station. Moment in the column is P·(3.5 − y), so it peaks at the lower root (y = −6.5) and falls to zero level with the load at y = +3.5. Sitting at y = −3.5 keeps 70 % of the peak while staying ~3 mm clear of the slot end and its stress concentration.
  • The inner holder grips the W from both faces, 3 mm above and 3 mm below, and closes into a box: its two side walls pass through the open slots, and a bridge behind the plate's bottom edge joins the two plates. Switch it off to see the plate and gauges it covers. It slides onto the W on a 0.2 mm fit per side and 0.15 mm per face — set for FDM printing — keeps 0.5 mm to the fixed outer arms, and stops 2 mm short of where the slots close. The two vertical edges at the mouth corners are rounded R2. The mouth also carries a 0.6 mm lead-in flare, which is not drawn here.
  • A 9 × 1 mm recess in each plate clears the gauge and carries its leads out through the rear face. Its edges sit mid-slot, 1.5 mm clear of the central column and 1.5 mm clear of the webs, so the inner holder never touches the member being measured — that one stays free to bend, and clamping is left to the webs and the bottom block. 2 mm of wall remains above and below each recess.
  • The outer holder is one piece: a C-jaw on each fixed arm — 4 mm of material above, below and outboard — joined by a yoke at the +y end. It is printed as part of the discant's housing, so the plate slides into it from −y until the plate's +y edge lands on the housing's pad, and a ⌀1.5 dowel pin across each jaw keeps it in. Two yokes bridge across the plate instead of reaching past it, so nothing projects beyond the +y edge. They stand off the plate faces at z = ±2.4, leaving a 4.8 mm gap for the leads to pass through, and sit in y at 5.5…12.5, filling the stretch between the inner holder and the plate edge down to a 1 mm gap. Using a pair rather than one ties the jaws together symmetrically. The jaw faces at x = 12 keep 0.5 mm to the inner holder, but the order is fixed: this part goes on first, since the yoke cannot pass over the 8.8 mm tall holder.
  • The leads run forward, in +y, two off each gauge. Each recess starts at y = −8, just behind its gauge, and opens at the inner holder's mouth; from there the leads lie on the plate, cross the open gap and leave through the gap between the outer holder's two yokes, standing 2 mm proud of its +y face. At ⌀0.7 they clear the recess ceiling and the yokes by about 0.45 mm. The inner holder's back wall is solid again, and it still clamps the bottom block over y = −12.7 … −8.
  • Orthographic or perspective. Parallel projection is the one to judge fits and alignment in — edges that should line up actually line up, and the preset views read as true elevations. Perspective is the natural-looking one. The switch keeps the apparent size, so the model does not jump.
  • The triad is the part's own frame, the one every dimension here is quoted in: origin at the centre of the plate in all three axes, X across the plate, Y towards the closed end, Z out of the gauged face. The plate therefore runs from z = −1.25 to +1.25, which is 1.25 mm below where the STEP file puts it; nothing else differs. Arrows are 20 mm long and drawn over the solids so they stay readable.
  • Two hairpin slots open at one edge, run the length of the plate, turn across the top and return to a closed end. Files: strain_gauge_plate.scad, inner_holder.scad and the matching STLs.
